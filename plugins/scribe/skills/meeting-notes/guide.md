# Shaping notes: contrasting examples

Each example shows what one reader needs, as a handful of `blocks`. Copy the thinking, not the layout.

## Executive call

Reader: someone who skims in a minute and acts on asks. Leave out the discussion.

- `decision`: "Pause the EU launch until Q1" (by Dana)
- `callout` risk: "Support headcount is unfunded if the launch moves back"
- `list`: asks of the reader, such as "Approve two contractors by Friday"

Todo actions: "Draft approval request", "Prepare agenda" for the follow-up.

## Product discussion

Reader: the team deciding what to build. They want the reasoning kept.

- `heading` "Problem", then a `paragraph`
- `list` of the options weighed, one line each with its main trade-off
- `decision`: the option chosen and why
- `list` under a `heading` "Open questions"

Todo actions: "Create ticket", "Draft spec outline".

## Sales or client call

Reader: the account owner preparing the next touch.

- `list`: pain in the client's own words, with a `quote` for the sharpest line
- `metric`: budget, with the timeline in its `note`
- `callout` risk: the objection that could stall the deal
- `decision`: the agreed next step

Todo actions: "Draft follow-up email", "Add lead to CRM" (drafts the record and says where it goes).

## Engineering or incident

Reader: the on-call engineer or whoever inherits the fix.

- `paragraph`: what broke and who noticed
- `metric`: impact, such as "42 min" with "checkout errors" as the note
- `paragraph`: the cause
- `decision`: the fix applied
- `list`: follow-ups

A `screen` block with the offset and a one-line caption marks an error page or dashboard that was shown. Todo actions: "Create ticket", "Draft incident summary".
